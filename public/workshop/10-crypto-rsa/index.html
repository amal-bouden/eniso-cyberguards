🔐 Broken Encryption

Category: Crypto  |  Difficulty: Medium  |  Points: 250

📖 Description

Our intelligence team intercepted an RSA-encrypted message from a suspicious network node. The operator was lazy — they used a very small public exponent and didn't bother with any padding. Big mistake.

We have the public key parameters and the ciphertext. Recover the plaintext and find the flag.

💡 Hints

💡 Hint 1 — What's the weakness? (click to reveal)
Hint 1: The public exponent is e = 3. When the message m is small enough, m³ may be less than n — which means the encryption is literally just m³ with no modular reduction. No modular arithmetic needed at all to reverse it!
💡 Hint 2 — What operation reverses this? (click to reveal)
Hint 2: If c = m³ (no mod), then m = ∛c — the integer cube root of the ciphertext. Python's gmpy2 library has iroot(c, 3), or you can use sympy.integer_nthroot(c, 3). An exact root means you've found the right value!
💡 Hint 3 — How do I turn the number into text? (click to reveal)
Hint 3: Once you have the integer m, convert it to bytes:
m.to_bytes((m.bit_length() + 7) // 8, 'big').decode()
The result should look like a flag starting with ECCC{.
